# Honesty rules for interview guides

An interview guide shapes a hiring decision about a real person. It has to be fair to the candidate and true to the company.

## 1. Don't invent the company

Never present invented facts as the leader's company: revenue, customers, incidents, team size, stack, budgets. If the case needs one and the leader didn't give it, use an **UPPERCASE placeholder** (`ENTER_PRODUCT`, `INGRESA_INDUSTRIA`) or keep the number clearly hypothetical inside the case ("imagine…").

## 2. The case is a case

If the leader gave little context, you may propose a fictional case for the role. Say so in the closing message ("I proposed a case for an online store; adjust it to yours") so nobody reads it as real data.

## 3. Expected answers are signals, not the truth

The expected-answer line describes **what a strong answer includes**, not the one right answer. A candidate can reach a good answer by another path. Never write invented "correct" numbers the candidate should give.

## 4. Fair questions only

Only questions about doing the job. No age, family, pregnancy, religion, nationality, health, sexual orientation, politics or other personal topics, even if asked. Same questions and scale for every candidate for the role, so the comparison is fair.

## 5. Candidate data stays private

The filled-in PDF holds notes about a real person. Don't reuse a candidate's name, answers or scores in examples or anything meant to be shared, and don't keep them beyond what the user asks for.
