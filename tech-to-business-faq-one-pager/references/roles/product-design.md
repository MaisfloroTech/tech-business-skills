# Product & Design

People who decide what gets built and how it feels. Their business levers: **building the right things, and making them usable enough that people adopt them.**

Family-wide ladder:

| Step | Ask for |
|---|---|
| Output | Specs, roadmaps, research sessions, designs, prototypes, design system components |
| Outcome | Adoption, task success, usability issues found/fixed, time on task, feature usage, experiment results |
| Business impact | Conversion, retention, revenue from features, support cost, building less of the wrong thing |

---

### Product Manager
- **Produces:** priorities, problem definitions, roadmap, decisions.
- **Serves:** customers, engineering, leadership.
- **If it stops:** teams build the wrong things, or build the right things too late.
- **Ask especially for:** outcomes of launched features vs the goal set before launch, experiment results, features killed early (money saved).
- **Typical risks:** a committed date at risk, low adoption of a launched feature, dependency on another team.
- **Red flag:** reporting features launched instead of the goal each one was supposed to move.

### Product Design
- **Produces:** end-to-end product experiences, flows and visual design.
- **Serves:** users, product managers, engineers.
- **If it stops:** inconsistent, confusing product; engineering builds without clear direction.
- **Ask especially for:** conversion or completion rate of redesigned flows, design system adoption (time saved per feature), usability issues solved.
- **Typical risks:** design debt, inconsistent experience across platforms, research skipped under deadline.
- **Red flag:** "redesigned X" without before/after behaviour.

### UX Design
- **Produces:** research, information architecture, usability improvements.
- **Serves:** users and product decisions.
- **If it stops:** decisions are based on opinions; usability problems reach production.
- **Ask especially for:** task success rate, time on task, drop-off points fixed, research findings that changed a decision (and what it saved).
- **Typical risks:** a key flow with known usability issues, research backlog nobody acts on.
- **Red flag:** "ran 12 interviews" without the decision they changed.
