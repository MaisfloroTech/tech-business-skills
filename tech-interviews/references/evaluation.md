# Evaluating the answers

Two rubrics, one per kind of question. Both score 1–4, so the average means the same across the interview.

## Technical questions (16): C · R · E · H

| Letter | English | Español | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C** | Correct | Correcto | Accurate, precise, no hand-waving or buzzwords used wrong |
| **R** | Reasoning | Razonamiento | Explains *why*, compares options, names the trade-off |
| **E** / **B** | Edge cases | Casos borde | Failure modes, limits, scale, security, what breaks first |
| **H** / **P** | Hands-on | Práctica | Has actually done it: real details, numbers, mistakes made and fixed |

In the Spanish PDF the letters are **C · R · B · P** (Correcto, Razonamiento, casos Borde, Práctica).

Tick each letter you heard. Then score:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **4 · Expert** | All four. Holds up and gets more specific when you dig two levels down. |
| **3 · Solid** | Correct and reasoned with real experience; misses some edge cases. |
| **2 · Basic** | Knows the concept or the tool, but can't explain why or hasn't used it in production. |
| **1 · Gaps** | Incorrect, vague or memorized; falls apart at the first follow-up. |

## The deep dive

Real expertise gets **more** specific as you go down:

1. **What** they'd do.
2. **How it works** underneath (the mechanism, not the brand).
3. **What breaks** and what they'd do then.

Memorized answers get vaguer at level 2. That difference is the main thing a technical interview is for.

## Business questions (4): STAR

| | | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Situation | A real case, with what was at stake for the business |
| **T** | Task | What was theirs to solve, and the goal |
| **A** | Action | What they did and why, options discarded |
| **R** | Result | What changed, in numbers, for customers, money, risk or time |

Score 4 when the STAR is complete and the result says what it meant for the business; 1 when it's generic or hypothetical.

## Red flags

- **Tool names instead of understanding.** "We used Kafka" with no idea why or how it delivers messages.
- **Never "I", always "we".** Ask what was theirs.
- **Never had a problem.** Everyone who ran something in production has broken it.
- **Can't say what they'd do differently.**
- **Only technical results in the business block.** "Latency went down" with no idea who noticed or what it was worth.
